A Template for Long-Form Writing

This page is a template, not a real post. Duplicate it for a new piece, replace the overline date and title above, and delete whatever below you don't need. The first paragraph always gets a drop cap; that's automatic, not something you add.

The lead paragraph of a piece can carry a drop cap, like this one does. It's a small flourish reserved for the very first letter of the very first paragraph, never used anywhere else in the piece, so it stays a flourish rather than a habit. Everything below is ordinary body text: the typeface, size, and line height that make up most of a reading page.

A Section Header #

Use a section header to mark a real turn in the piece: a new movement in an essay, a new act in a longer narrative. Give it an id and a hand-written # link like the one above if you want it to be a real deep link; otherwise just leave the anchor off.

A Sub-Header, for a Smaller Division

A sub-header sits one step down from a section header, useful for breaking up a long section without implying a whole new movement. Inline text can carry bold emphasis, italic emphasis, or a link out to something else, all without leaving the reading measure.

A block quote is a quotation from somewhere else, not your own words. It sits in the secondary type used for chrome elsewhere on the site, with a red rule on the left, and stays plainly attributed.

Lists, When They're Useful

Ordinary lists are available too, for when a piece genuinely calls for one rather than for decoration:

  • An unordered item, for points with no inherent order.
  • A second item, same weight as the first.
  • A third, if three is what the thought needs.
  1. An ordered item, for steps or a real sequence.
  2. A second step that follows from the first.
  3. A third and final step.
